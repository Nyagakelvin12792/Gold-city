# Phase 4: The Living Auction

## Finding Value
Value is an emergent property of participant agreement, not the same thing as price. Price is where the last trade happened. Value is where most participants agree business should keep happening.

## Fair Value vs. Unfair Value
**Fair value:** where most buyers and sellers agree business can comfortably occur — high volume, high time allocation.
**Unfair value:** prices that severely favor one side, causing rapid rejection or aggressive flow driving price away fast.

## Balance
Two-sided auction symmetry. Buyers and sellers evenly matched — the market rotates and accumulates inventory rather than trending.

## Imbalance
One-sided auction. Demand exceeds supply, or vice versa. The market enters trend discovery, searching for new value.

## Rotation
Price moving back and forth within an established value area. Routine business, nothing structural changing.

## Migration
Value relocation. The Point of Control and Value Area shift to a new price district entirely.

## Responsive vs. Initiative Activity
**Responsive:** buying below value or selling above it — mean-reversion behavior.
**Initiative:** aggressively buying above value or selling below it, pushing into new territory — trend-initiating behavior.

## Exhaustion
Aggressive order flow running out of fuel as price approaches key passive liquidity walls.

## Continuation
Initiative flow absorbing all passive opposition and maintaining momentum through LVN highways.

## Failure
An initiative move that fails to secure acceptance, leading to sharp responsive traps and structural failure — snapping back into the old value area.

## Acceptance After Migration
The confirmation phase where an imbalance move halts, consolidates, and forms a new HVN — confirming the migration actually held rather than just poking through.
